# Recovery when both individual games point to the wrong person

Registered before opening the longer-training study's new decision records. This diagnostic uses the accepted full scorer and the same official questions already evaluated. It asks what additional observed play can do that a longer checkpoint on the same single game cannot show.

Use the first two held-out games in assigned slot order, exactly the two games making the official two-game query. Evaluate the accepted full model on its two individual games and their joint query. In splits 4 and 5 (primary), stratify by whether both single queries are wrong, one is right, or both are right. Within the both-wrong stratum prespecify agreement versus disagreement of the two incorrect top-1 nominees. Report numerators, denominators, paired owner-clustered intervals, and which part of the total joint minus random-single top-1 improvement comes from each stratum. A joint win when both singles are wrong cannot be obtained by selecting either single winner. Repeat on splits 0–3. Count jointly correct cases when neither single had truth in its top two. Use the original accepted committee for these selection and scoring statements. This comparison uses more decisions, and two games known to have the same owner.

For difficulty sensitivity, within each pool/seed form six unordered strata from the two original true-rank bands (second, third–fifth, sixth or lower). Among both-wrong pairs, match the different-rival group to the same-rival group's true-rank distribution on common support. Report supported counts, the standardized difference in joint accuracy, and owner-bootstrap intervals with usable bootstrap draws. As a descriptive cross-check on the orthogonal role of longer training, report the registered addition's own one-versus-two-game gain and, on the very same accepted both-wrong subset, the rate at which at least one of the two singles becomes correct with longer training. This diagnostic is distinct from a deployable single-query improvement.
