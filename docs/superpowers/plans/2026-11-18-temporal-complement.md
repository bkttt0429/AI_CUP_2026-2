# A complementary temporal direction for the actual nominees

Registered after the bilateral comparison reproduced identity information in projected temporal traces but the scalar reranker failed. This is a new, exploratory screen on splits already examined. Preserve reference/query roles, nominee folds, score scale, one nonnegative calibration coefficient and adoption threshold from the first protocol. Compare actual finalist pairs on all 4,800 held decisions. Keep the existing 368 bilateral pairs as a descriptive mechanism check.

For each game and probe subtract the mean of its four deletion embeddings from each deletion embedding, making a four-by-128 trace. For two finalists, compute the difference of their unit-normalized full-game reference centroids, normalize that direction, and remove from each trace its projection on this axis. Learn each finalist's mean remaining trace from seven reference games. Score the query against their difference, with a midpoint correction. Estimate the squared norm of each person's population mean in this correction by averaging inner products from distinct reference games (42 ordered pairs). Average margins from the two probes. The primary arm uses the perpendicular component. Controls use the whole trace, only the parallel component of the same model, and the perpendicular component under the matched shuffled masks. Test numerically that whole equals parallel plus perpendicular and swapping the two people negates every margin.

Report paired competition effects, the primary minus its shuffled control, and the primary's standalone accuracies among true winners, true runners and in the bilateral sample. The projection removes one specific intact axis per probe and pair. Remaining coordinates can still encode correlated whole-game information. No selection or weight search across residual directions. A +0.002 ranking gain with positive lower confidence bound for the perpendicular arm would trigger frozen ranking confirmation; diagnostic bilateral accuracy alone cannot do so.
